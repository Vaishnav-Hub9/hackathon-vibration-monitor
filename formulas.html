1. Fast Fourier Transform — Cooley–Tukey Butterfly lib/fft.ts

The Fourier Transform converts a time-domain vibration signal into its frequency components — the machine's "fingerprint":

$$X[k] = \sum_{n=0}^{N-1} x[n]\, e^{-i 2\pi k n / N}, \qquad k = 0 \dots N-1$$

The butterfly trick splits the sum into even/odd samples and reuses partial results:

$$X[k] = X_{even}[k] + W_N^k\, X_{odd}[k] \qquad X[k + N/2] = X_{even}[k] - W_N^k\, X_{odd}[k]$$

with the twiddle factor:

$$W_N^k = e^{-i 2\pi k / N} = \cos\!\left(\tfrac{2\pi k}{N}\right) - i \sin\!\left(\tfrac{2\pi k}{N}\right)$$

Why it matters: naive DFT is $O(N^2)$; the butterfly recursion makes it $O(N \log N)$. For 2048 points: ~4.2M → ~22K operations (~180× faster), which is what makes live analysis possible. We precompute the cos/sin tables and AC-couple the signal (subtract the mean) so the accelerometer's +1 g gravity offset doesn't dominate bin 0.

5. Wavelet Decomposition — Daubechies-4, Level 4 15 features

The signal is passed through low-pass (approximation) and high-pass (detail) filters, downsampled at each level:

$$x[n] \;\longrightarrow\; \{\,cA_4,\; cD_4,\; cD_3,\; cD_2,\; cD_1\,\}$$

For each of the 5 coefficient sets we store mean, std, and energy → 15 features total.

Why it matters: Fourier gives frequency only; wavelets give frequency + time location — they capture the brief "impact" transients of a chipped ball that the FFT smears across the spectrum.

6. Bearing Fault Frequencies — BPFO the physics

Ball-Pass Frequency of the Outer race — the rate at which balls pass a defect on the outer ring:

$$BPFO = \frac{n}{2}\, f_r \left(1 - \frac{d}{D}\cos\phi\right)$$
SymbolMeaning
$n$number of balls
$f_r$rotation frequency (Hz)
$d$ball diameter
$D$pitch diameter
$\phi$contact angle

For our bearing this lands at ~157 Hz — so a spectral peak there means outer-race spalling. The dashboard's BPFO score is the real spectral energy ratio in the fault band (130–180 Hz):

$$BPFO_{score} = \frac{\sum_{f \in [130,180]} |X(f)|}{\sum_f |X(f)|}$$
Judge line: "A defect on the outer race makes the balls pass over it at a mathematically predictable rate — ~157 Hz for our bearing. When the FFT shows a peak there, the machine is telling us its outer race is spalled."

8. Remaining Useful Life — Linear Least-Squares Regression routes/machines.ts

Fit the real decay trend of stored health history, then project to the failure threshold:

$$\text{slope} = \frac{n\sum xy - \sum x \sum y}{n\sum x^2 - (\sum x)^2} \qquad \text{intercept} = \frac{\sum y - \text{slope}\sum x}{n}$$
$$\text{health}(t) = \text{intercept} + \text{slope}\cdot t \qquad \text{clamped to } [20, 100]$$

Why it matters: slope is clamped to $[-1.2,\,0]$ so the model never shows false recovery — the projection only decays. Each machine gets its own trend from its own data; no hardcoded rates.

🎤 60-Second Presentation Script

"Under the hood it's classic signal processing plus a trained classifier. We take 2048 vibration samples and run a Cooley–Tukey FFT — the butterfly algorithm, O(N log N) — to see the machine's frequency fingerprint. From time and frequency domains plus wavelet decomposition we extract ~37 features: RMS, kurtosis for impact spikes, crest factor, spectral entropy, and the bearing fault frequencies like BPFO at 157 Hz. Those features feed our trained model, which outputs the fault and a confidence. Health comes straight from that confidence, and RUL is a least-squares regression of the health history projected to the failure threshold. The one-liner: Fourier + wavelets + a trained classifier — all live, all real."
